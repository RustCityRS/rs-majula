Last updated 23 October 2026.

Most of this site recreates the RuneScape website as it was in 2004, and this page used to show Jagex's privacy policy from that time. That policy was about Jagex's own services: their billing partners, their adverts and their offices. None of it described this server. We've replaced it with our own policy below, which covers what this server actually does.

This website and its game worlds are run independently and have no connection to Jagex. This page covers what we keep about you when you make an account, play, or use the support pages, why we keep it, how long for, and who can see it.

We don't sell your information, we don't show adverts, and we don't use any tracking or analytics services.

This server is run by Rust City, from the United States. Rust City is responsible for everything this page describes (the "controller", in the words of data protection law). You can reach us at contact@rustcity.org.

{% endcall %} {% call section("Your account") %}

To make an account you choose a username and a password. That's all we ask for: no email address, real name or date of birth. Your password is never stored as you typed it. It's hashed with Argon2id, using a random salt for each password and a pepper: a secret key that only our game and website servers know, kept out of the database. A hash can't be turned back into the password, not even by staff, and someone who got hold of a copy of our database couldn't even start guessing passwords without that key as well.

With the account we keep the date it was made, the internet address it was made from, any staff role it has, and whether it's banned or muted and until when.

We keep your game save: your stats, items, bank, quest progress, where you are, your appearance and your game settings. We keep your friends and ignore lists too. We also keep your earlier saves for 30 days, so staff can put your account back if it was hijacked or something went wrong. The newest 5 are kept however old they are.

The hiscores show your username and your stats to everyone. Staff accounts aren't listed.

{% endcall %} {% call section("When you log in") %}

With each login we keep the time, the world, and these details about your connection:

  • Your internet (IP) address.
  • A browser ID. The web client makes up a random number the first time it runs, keeps it in your browser's local storage, and sends it when you log in. It's only for telling browsers apart, and it's gone if you clear your site data.
  • If you play in the browser, what your browser says about itself: its user agent, its platform (such as "Windows"), its preferred language, and whether it looks like a phone or tablet. Browsers can claim anything, so staff treat this as a description, not proof.

We keep these for 180 days. They let staff see when accounts share an address or browser, which is how we find ban evasion, bot farms, and the accounts a hijacker came from. Many people share an address (a household, a school, a mobile network), and staff know that, so a shared address on its own is never treated as proof of anything.

Failed login attempts are counted by address for a minute, to slow down password guessing, and then forgotten.

{% endcall %} {% call section("Session recordings") %}

Every game session on our worlds is recorded on the server. This is the part people ask about most, so here is exactly what that means.

The game client and the world talk to each other in small messages called packets. The world sends packets that tell your client what to show: the map, the players and NPCs near you, your inventory, chat and interfaces. Your client sends packets back when you do something: clicking to walk, choosing a menu option, typing a chat message. The client also reports some input on its own, as the original game did, for example samples of where your mouse moved and clicked, the camera angle, whether the game window has focus, and on some game versions which keys were pressed. As those packets pass through the world, it writes them down with a timestamp. That stream is the recording. It's compressed and stored in our database, at a few kilobytes per minute of play.

What a recording does not contain:

  • Anything from outside the game: no screenshots, no other windows or tabs, no camera or microphone, nothing about the rest of your device. Nothing runs on your computer apart from the game client, and that's the same client everybody plays with.
  • Your password. It's sent once, encrypted, when you log in, and recording starts after that.

To watch a recording, the staff console runs the normal game client and feeds it the recorded packets in place of a live world. Staff see roughly what your client would have shown. It's a rebuilt view of the game, not a video of your screen. Staff can also watch a session that's still going, a couple of seconds behind.

Why we record:

  • Bots and macros. Automated checks read the same stream as it's recorded, look at how a session plays, and score it. A high score puts the session in front of a staff member. It doesn't ban anyone by itself. We don't publish how the checks work, because that would only help people get around them.
  • Disputes. Scams, "my account was hijacked", items that went missing, harassment. A recording shows what actually happened, which is fairer than one person's word against another's.
  • Bugs. When something breaks in the game, a recording shows what led up to it.

Recordings are deleted after 7 days. When a staff member saves part of one as evidence in a moderation case, that part is kept with the case.

One check acts by itself: if a client sends something the real client can't send, the account is banned for a short time automatically. If you think that was a mistake, appeal it and a member of staff will look at it. Every other ban is decided by a person, and you can appeal those too.

{% endcall %} {% call section("Chat and game activity") %}

We log the chat you send, public and private, as you typed it (before the word filter), along with where you were and who a private message went to. We also log trades, duel stakes, items you drop or pick up, and some other game events. These help staff deal with scams, rule breaking and lost items, and let us undo the damage when an account is cleaned out. They're kept for 90 days.

{% endcall %} {% call section("Reports, appeals and recovery") %}

When you report abuse we keep who reported whom, the rule, when, and where. When you appeal a ban or mute we keep your statement and the address you sent it from. When you ask for an account to be recovered we keep what you tell us on the form, plus your address, browser ID and browser details from the request, so staff can compare them with the account's past logins. When staff send you a message in the Message Centre we keep it, and when you first read it, until you delete your account.

These records, staff's decisions on them, and the moderation cases built from them are kept for as long as they're needed to run moderation fairly: to enforce a ban, to handle an appeal later, and to see someone's history. The commands staff use in game are kept too, so staff can be held to account.

{% endcall %} {% call section("Who can see it") %}

Only staff, through a private staff console that isn't reachable from the public internet. What they can see depends on their role. Moderators see stand-in labels in place of internet addresses, which lets them tell when two accounts share an address without seeing the address itself. Admins and above see the addresses.

Staff can't look into their own account, or into staff above their role. Opening a recording, looking someone up, and every other staff action is written to an audit log that more senior staff can read.

{% endcall %} {% call section("Your browser") %}

This website doesn't set any cookies. The game client keeps a few things in your browser's local storage: the browser ID above, and your display settings (window size, scaling, and the phone keyboard mode). It also keeps the game's files in your browser's storage so they don't download again each time. You can clear all of this from your browser's site settings at any time.

{% endcall %} {% call section("Services we use") %}

We rent our servers from netcup. We send server metrics (such as tick times and request counts) and our servers' own logs to Grafana Cloud, so we can tell when something is wrong. Those logs include connection addresses, and sometimes usernames. Nobody else gets your information unless the law requires it.

Everything we keep is stored in the United States: our servers are in netcup's US data centre, and our Grafana Cloud account is in a US region. If you play from another country, your information is sent to the US to be stored there.

{% endcall %} {% call section("Why we're allowed to") %}

If you're in the EU or the UK, data protection law needs a reason for each thing we do with your information. Ours are:

  • Running the game you signed up for. Your account, password hash, game save, friends and ignore lists, and the hiscores. Without these there's no game to play.
  • Keeping the game fair and safe (our "legitimate interests"). Login records, session recordings and the checks on them, chat and activity logs, reports, appeals, recovery requests, moderation cases, and our servers' logs. We've weighed these against your privacy, which is why recordings last only a week, moderators don't see internet addresses, and every look staff take is logged.
  • The law. If we're legally required to hand something over, we do.
{% endcall %} {% call section("Your rights") %}

You can ask us to:

  • Give you a copy of what we hold about you.
  • Correct something that's wrong.
  • Delete your account and what goes with it.
  • Hold off using your information while a complaint about it is sorted out.
  • Send you your data (your account and save) in a form a computer can read.
  • Stop anything we do under "keeping the game fair and safe". We'll look at your reasons. Recordings and checks only work if they cover everyone, so we may not be able to switch them off for one account while it still plays, and if so we'll tell you why.
  • Have a person review the automatic ban described under Session recordings.

You can do the most common of these yourself, straight away, on your account page: see your recent logins, change your password, download a copy of your data, or delete your account.

For anything else, email us at contact@rustcity.org, or ask a staff member on our Discord. We'll need you to prove the account is yours first, and we'll answer within a month. When we delete an account its save, logins, recordings, chat and logs go with it. We may keep a record of a ban or an open case for as long as it's still needed, for example so a ban can't be dodged by deleting the account and making it again.

If you're in the EU or the UK and you're not happy with how we've handled your information, you can complain to the data protection authority where you live: the ICO in the UK, or your country's authority in the EU. We'd rather you told us first, so we can try to put it right.

The game isn't meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly keep accounts for them. If you think a child under 13 has an account, tell us and we'll delete it.

When we change what we collect, or how long we keep it, we'll update this page and post about it in the news.
